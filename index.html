🎛️ Platform Controls Worth Turning On

  • YouTube / YouTube Kids: Use YouTube Kids for under-9s; for older kids, turn on Restricted Mode and review watch history together weekly — autoplay off.
  • TikTok: Family Pairing links your account to theirs: screen-time limits, Restricted Mode, and DMs off/limited (DMs are disabled under 16 by default — keep it that way).
  • Roblox / games: Set the account birthdate correctly (it drives chat filters), enable Account Restrictions, and turn on the account PIN so settings can't be changed back.
  • WhatsApp & messaging: Not for under-13s (platform rule). For teens: profile photo to contacts only, and agree that unknown group invites get shown to a parent first.
  • The device itself: Use the platform family tools — Google Family Link (Android/Chromebook) or Apple Screen Time — for app approvals, bedtime downtime and location you both agree on.
  • App-store gate: Require your approval (and your password, not theirs) for every new app install. One setting prevents a whole category of surprises.

💬 Conversation Starters (that don't start a war)

  • "Show me your favorite creator/game right now — I want to see what you love about it." (Interest before inspection builds the trust you'll need later.)
  • "If someone online asked for a photo of you, what would you do?" (Agree the answer together: stop, don't reply, tell an adult — and it's never the child's fault.)
  • "Has anything online ever made you feel weird or upset?" (Ask calmly, more than once, in low-pressure moments like car rides.)
  • "Let's make our family rule together: what should need a parent's OK?" (Buying, new apps, new contacts, sharing photos, location. Written rules feel fairer than ambush rules.)
  • "If you ever mess up online, you can tell me and we'll fix it together — you won't be in trouble for telling me." (This single promise is what makes children report grooming and bullying early instead of hiding them.)
